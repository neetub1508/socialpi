The trade is real, and it is operational rather than technical: you become the sysadmin. Everything below is about whether that trade is worth taking, and how to structure it so it stays reversible.

Method

Think in capabilities, not providers

The logical architecture does not change with the provider — the services and their connections are identical everywhere. What changes is whether a provider can satisfy each requirement.

R3 and R4 are what eliminated Railway. Any provider that gives you a real VM or bare metal satisfies both automatically, because you own the machine.

Hetzner's 2026 price rises — read before assuming

Hetzner raised cloud prices three times in 2026, and the increases were not uniform:

  • April 2026 — broad rise of up to ~37–43%, applied to existing customers too.
  • 15 June 2026 — CPX and CCX hit hard: CPX22 €7.99 → €19.49 (+144%), CCX13 €15.99 → €42.99 (+169%).
  • CAX (ARM) rose only ~30–33% and remains the cheapest cloud line.
  • Dedicated (AX) was far less affected — AX42 is €49/month + €39 one-off setup.

Two practical consequences. Do not build a cost model on CPX/CCX — they are no longer the value play; CAX or AX are. And existing servers keep their price, but a rescale counts as a new order at current rates, so size with headroom rather than planning to resize.

Egress is the sleeper cost

A product that uploads video is egress-heavy by nature. 1 TB/month costs $100 on Railway and €0 on Hetzner. Over a year that single difference exceeds the entire Hetzner bill. The per-user costs — $0.015 per X post, $0.20 with a link, plus tokens and image generation — are unchanged by provider and are covered in the deployment document.

Principle

The rule that keeps this reversible

Depend on no provider-specific primitive. Everything in this stack is OCI containers plus PostgreSQL plus Valkey — nothing else. That means:

  • No Railway-specific networking, no Vercel-only rendering features, no AWS Lambda handlers, no proprietary queue service.
  • The deployment is a Docker Compose file plus environment variables. Moving providers means bringing up the same compose file elsewhere and restoring the database.
  • Coolify is Apache-2.0 and only a control plane — if you abandon it, the containers keep running.

This is the practical meaning of deployment-first: the architecture must not encode where it runs. Get that right and the provider decision stops being permanent — which matters more than getting it perfect now, given how much European hosting repriced this year.

Recommendation

Start on one Hetzner AX42 with Coolify and Neon Postgres, at roughly $75/month. About a third of the Railway cost, removes both capability blockers, includes 20 TB of egress, and keeps every future option open — including self-hosting E2B on the very same machine, since bare metal already provides KVM.

Revisit if India or the US becomes the primary market (latency argues for in-region), if revenue starts depending on uptime (move to Stage 3, or buy managed hosting as insurance), or if you cannot staff the sysadmin duty (go back to managed and treat the extra ~$150/month as operational cover).
